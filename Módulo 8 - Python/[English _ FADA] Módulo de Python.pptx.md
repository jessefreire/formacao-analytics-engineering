#### **K-MEANS CLUSTERING** 

To determine which cluster an observation belongs to, it is necessary to identify a representative midpoint of the cluster, known as the centroid. 

1. Choosing the number of clusters (K): one of the most commonly used techniques for determining the appropriate number of _clusters_ is the _elbow method;_ 

2. Initialization of centroids: K points are randomly placed in the data space; 

3. Assigning observations to _clusters_ : each observation is assigned to the nearest cluster center based on distance metrics, typically the Euclidean distance; 

4. Recalculation of cluster centers: K-Means is an iterative algorithm, so new cluster centers are calculated based on the average distance between the observations in the cluster. As the cluster centers are repositioned, the observations are assigned to their nearest cluster centers; 



5. Convergence: Steps 3 and 4 are repeated until the center points no longer change.
